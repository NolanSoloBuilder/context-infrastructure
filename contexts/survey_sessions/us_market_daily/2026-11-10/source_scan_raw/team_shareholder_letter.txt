The System of Work for humans and agents
For nearly 25 years, we've helped teams plan, build, and move work forward. More than 350,000 customers, including 85% of the Fortune 500, have run billions of workflows on Atlassian. That's given us an unmatched understanding of how teams actually work, from how they make decisions, to how they hand off projects and get things done.
Now, teams are changing. It’s no longer just humans, but humans and agents working side by side.
Agents are a powerful multiplier. They help people move faster and take on work that was once out of reach. But more agents means more output, and more output means more complexity. The companies that pull ahead in the AI era will connect their people and agents on a trusted platform that helps them work together and manage that complexity.
That's what our System of Work delivers. Every business is ultimately a network of interconnected processes. How effectively people and agents execute those processes together determines what the business achieves. Our platform helps customers drive faster, more reliable outcomes by bringing together three reinforcing capabilities:
Context
that
makes agents useful
Collaboration
that lets humans and agents work as one team on a shared platform
Confidence
to scale AI with the right governance and controls
Each one makes the others stronger.
The Atlassian Platform
Context
AI models are getting smarter every day, but
context
is what makes them useful at work.
Across billions of workflows, our platform captures the goals teams set, the work they take on, the knowledge they create, the decisions they make, the code they ship, and the outcomes they achieve. All of this comes together in the
Teamwork Graph
, a living memory of each customer's organization that no off-the-shelf model can replicate.
The Graph spans seven types of context: knowledge, work, people, communications, code, assets, and now, data. It maps more than
250 billion objects and relationships
, up from 150 billion six months ago, and connects to more than 80 third-party tools out of the box. Customers are putting that breadth to work. More than 60% connect four or more types of context, which gives their agents a more complete picture of how work gets done.
Any agent or coding assistant can now tap into the Teamwork Graph via the Atlassian MCP Server and the Teamwork Graph CLI, and developers are taking notice. Atlassian’s Teamwork Graph CLI usage grew
more than
250% month-over-month in September alone
.
We're bringing context to the tools teams already use. We expanded our partnership with OpenAI, connecting
ChatGPT
and
Codex
to the Teamwork Graph so teams get AI grounded in their real work. This strengthens Atlassian's role as the context layer and helps enterprises see real ROI from AI.
We're also helping customers get there faster. This quarter, we expanded our Forward Deployed Engineering (FDE) program to scale enterprise AI transformation. Our FDEs help connect more systems to further enrich organizations' context, build custom Rovo agents and automations, and streamline workflows across technical and non-technical teams.  Expedia and  Reddit are already seeing the value.
All this context is paying off for our business. Customers with a rich graph are
twice as likely
to become deep Rovo adopters, and those adopters are growing ARR
more than 2x faster
than customers with light usage. Simply put, the more customers connect, the more value they get.
“The power of AI isn’t just in parsing words. It’s in the graph. By connecting Rovo to our entire ecosystem, including Jira, Confluence, Slack, and Google, we’ve turned the Teamwork Graph into a real-time engine for delivery. Atlassian provides the context we need to make AI work for us. Collaboration
The future of work isn't people and agents working in isolation, and it isn't agents replacing people. It's teams of humans and agents working toward shared goals, with clear ownership, visible handoffs, and shared context. It's AI moving from single-player productivity, to multiplayer teamwork across the organization.
That's why we introduced the
Agentic
Multiplayer Protocol
(AMP)
at Team '26 Europe last month.
AMP
is the multiplayer collaboration layer, built into the Atlassian platform, that lets humans and agents work together. With
AMP
, agents become visible teammates. Whether they're working on their own or on someone's behalf, they can create, review, collaborate, and act
alongside
the people they work with - in real time or asynchronously.

And because AMP runs on the Teamwork Graph, every handoff, review, and decision adds context back into the system.

Innovation across the Atlassian platform
This quarter marked another major leap forward across the Atlassian platform, led by the introduction of AMP, and expansion of our context, collaboration, and governance layers for teams of humans and agents.
AMP
Built into the Atlassian platform, AMP makes agents visible teammates that work alongside people across Atlassian and third-party apps, each with a distinct identity, clear attribution, and governed authority.

applications

Insights

Redesigned Atlassian MCP Server

to build a service desk from a plain-language description.

FY27 Outlook
With momentum across our priorities and focused execution on driving durable, profitable growth, we continue to take a thoughtful and prudent approach in setting our outlook, which contemplates the ongoing macroeconomic, fiscal, and geopolitical uncertainty, and the continued scaling of our enterprise go-to-market engine.
Subscription ARR and Total Revenue
We expect Subscription ARR to grow approximately 18.5% y/y as of the end of FY27 and total revenue to grow approximately 13.75% y/y in FY27, driven by continued Cloud momentum and healthy Data Center retention.
As a reminder, our announcement of Data Center’s end-of-life in March 2029 (DC EOL) pulled customer purchases forward and caused us to recognize a higher proportion of Data Center contract value as upfront term-license revenue in FY26. The year-over-year comparison will weigh on FY27 Data Center revenue and total revenue growth, but we expect total revenue growth to re-accelerate in FY28 as we lap these effects.
Subscription ARR offers a supplemental view of our underlying business, as it normalizes variable revenue patterns and the timing effects of ASC 606.
Cloud revenue
We expect Cloud revenue to grow approximately 26.0% y/y in FY27,
driven by customers broadening their adoption of our System of Work as they add new users and teams to our core offerings, adopt our Collections to deploy Rovo agents across their workflows, and upgrade to higher-value editions to take advantage of the advanced capabilities and innovation of the Atlassian platform.
We continue to expect migrations from Data Center to Cloud to play out over a multi-year period and to contribute mid-to-high single-digits of Cloud revenue growth in FY27.
In terms of seasonality, we expect 1H growth rates to be higher than 2H due to tougher comparisons in 2H, including the impact from the DX acquisition which closed in Q2’26.
Data Center revenue
We expect Data Center revenue to decline approximately (16.0%) y/y in FY27,
driven by lapping the revenue recognition impacts from the DC EOL announcement, customer purchasing activity that was pulled into FY26 from FY27, continued migrations to Cloud, and lower seat expansion as Data Center customers plan their Cloud migration. These will be partially offset by pricing and Data Center renewals, including customers adopting hybrid deployment strategies, allowing them to migrate over time.
Marketplace and other revenue
We expect Marketplace and other revenue to grow approximately 15.0% y/y,
driven by sales of third-party marketplace apps for our Cloud and Data Center offerings. As a reminder, we currently have a lower Marketplace take rate on the sale of third-party Cloud apps relative to Data Center apps as we incentivize further development on our Forge platform
Gross margin
We expect GAAP gross margin of 84.5% and non-GAAP gross margin of 86.5%,
driven by growing usage of Rovo and hosting costs as more of our business shifts to Cloud. We continue to optimize our cloud infrastructure through our platform investments, and manage AI workloads through our AI Gateway, which matches each AI task to the best-fit model for quality and cost.
Operating margin
We expect GAAP operating margin of 5.0% and non-GAAP operating margin of 25.5%.
FY26 non-GAAP operating margin benefited by approximately four percentage points from the timing of Data Center revenue recognition following the DC EOL announcement. For FY27, we’ve shifted compensation toward cash and away from equity as part of our work to reduce stock-based compensation as a percentage of revenue, which is expected to lower non-GAAP operating margin by approximately three percentage points. Adjusting for these effects, we anticipate an increase in non-GAAP operating margin in FY27 as compared to FY26. We remain focused on GAAP profitability and margin expansion over time while investing judiciously in AI and enterprise sales.
Share count
We expect our net diluted share count to remain relatively flat in FY27 versus FY26. We expect to generate healthy free cash flow, allowing us to opportunistically offset dilution and return capital to stockholders.

ABOUT NON-GAAP FINANCIAL MEASURES AND OTHER FINANCIAL MEASURES
In addition to the measures presented in our condensed consolidated financial statements, we regularly review other measures that are not presented in accordance with GAAP, defined as non-GAAP financial measures by the SEC, to evaluate our business, measure our performance, identify trends, prepare financial forecasts and make strategic decisions. The key measures we consider are non-GAAP gross profit and non-GAAP gross margin, non-GAAP operating income and non-GAAP operating margin, non-GAAP net income, non-GAAP net income per diluted share and free cash flow (collectively, the Non-GAAP Financial Measures).
These Non-GAAP Financial Measures, which may be different from similarly titled non-GAAP measures used by other companies, provide supplemental information regarding our operating performance on a non-GAAP basis that excludes certain gains, losses and charges of a non-cash nature or that occur relatively infrequently and/or that management considers to be unrelated to our core operations. Management believes that tracking and presenting these Non-GAAP Financial Measures provides management, our board of directors, investors and the analyst community with the ability to better evaluate matters such as: our ongoing core operations, including comparisons between periods and against other companies in our industry; our ability to generate cash to service our debt and fund our operations; and the underlying business trends that are affecting our performance.
Our Non-GAAP Financial Measures include:
Non-GAAP gross profit and non-GAAP gross margin.
Excludes expenses related to stock-based compensation, amortization of acquired intangible assets, and restructuring charges.
Non-GAAP operating income and non-GAAP operating margin.
Excludes expenses related to stock-based compensation, amortization of acquired intangible assets, and restructuring charges.
Non-GAAP net income and non-GAAP net income per diluted share.
Excludes expenses related to stock-based compensation, amortization of acquired intangible assets, restructuring charges, and the related income tax effects of these items.
Free cash flow.
Free cash flow is defined as net cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditures, which consists of purchases of property and equipment.
We understand that although these Non-GAAP Financial Measures are frequently used by investors and the analyst community in their evaluation of our financial performance, these measures have limitations as analytical tools, and you should not consider them in isolation or as substitutes for analysis of our results as reported under GAAP. We compensate for such limitations by reconciling these Non-GAAP Financial Measures to the most comparable GAAP financial measures.
We encourage you to review the tables in this shareholder letter titled “Reconciliation of GAAP to Non-GAAP Results” and “Reconciliation of GAAP to Non-GAAP Financial Targets” that present such reconciliations.
We define annual recurring revenue (“ARR”) as the annualized recurring run-rate revenue of subscription agreements to our Cloud and Data Center offerings at a point in time. We calculate ARR by taking the monthly recurring revenue (“MRR”) run-rate for Cloud and Data Center subscriptions and multiplying it by 12. Cloud MRR for each month is calculated by aggregating monthly recurring revenue from committed contractual amounts at a point in time. Data Center MRR for each month is calculated based on the annual contract value from committed contractual amounts at a point in time. ARR on a single product basis is defined as ARR from subscriptions for that specific product. ARR and MRR should be viewed independently of revenue and do not represent our revenue under GAAP, as they are operational metrics that can be affected by contract start and end dates and renewal rates.
We calculate net revenue retention rate (NRR) at a point in time by dividing monthly recurring revenue (MRR) at the end of a reporting period (Current Period MRR) by the MRR for the same group of customers at the end of the prior 12-month period. Current Period MRR includes existing customer expansion net of existing customer contraction and attrition but excludes MRR from new customers in the current period.
We define Subscription ARR as the annual recurring revenue from subscription agreements to our Cloud and Data Center offerings at a point in time. It reflects the annualized value of active subscriptions, including recurring revenue from upgrades and add-ons, but excludes one-time fees. For monthly subscriptions, ARR is calculated by multiplying monthly recurring revenue (MRR) by 12. ARR should be viewed independently of revenue and does not represent our revenue under GAAP, as it is an operational metric that can be affected by contract start and end dates, renewal rates, and the timing of invoices.

Momentum across our strategic priorities
Expanding the System of Work with our customers.
By bringing work, knowledge, people, and agents together on our unified platform, organizations are collaborating in multi-player mode with shared context and trusted governance. With team collaboration increasingly including agents, we’ve now introduced AMP to further progress our System of Work. Today, over 10 million human-agent collaborations occur on our platform each month. The momentum in our business shows this approach is working, as over 3,000 customers have upgraded to Teamwork Collection, up 7x y/y, and over 70,000 customers have now adopted Service Collection, with its ARR continuing to grow north of 30% y/y and competitive displacements up 44% y/y.
AI strengthens the platform opportunity.
Decades of collaboration across the Atlassian platform have compounded into a rich context graph of how teams get work done, one that is unique to every customer and gives AI a proprietary foundation that is difficult to replicate. That foundation keeps growing, with the
Teamwork Graph now connecting over 250 billion objects and relationship
s, up from 150 billion six months ago, and reaching beyond Atlassian through more than 275 third-party connectors. That context is also paying off for the business. Customers with a richer graph are
twice as likely
to become deep Rovo adopters, and those adopters are growing ARR
more than 2x faster
than customers with light usage.
Delivering on enterprise demand.
Enterprises need a platform that connects work across teams of humans and agents while respecting permissions and governance requirements. We’ve built the controls and visibility that enable organizations to scale AI with confidence, and those platform investments are paying off, as now more than 87% of enterprise Cloud customers are using Rovo. This is reflected in our results, with RPO growing 44% y/y to $4.7 billion and current RPO growing 28%, fueled by larger and longer customer commitments to the Atlassian platform.
Driving durable profitable growth
Gross margin and operating margin both landed ahead of expectations, driven by revenue outperformance, leverage from platform investments, and lower-than-expected operating costs, reflecting our increased focus on operational discipline to support durable, profitable growth.

.

FY27 Outlook
With momentum across our priorities and focused execution on driving durable, profitable growth, we continue to take a thoughtful and prudent approach in setting our outlook, which contemplates the ongoing macroeconomic, fiscal, and geopolitical uncertainty, and the continued scaling of our enterprise go-to-market engine.
Subscription ARR and Total Revenue
We expect Subscription ARR to grow approximately 18.5% y/y as of the end of FY27 and total revenue to grow approximately 13.75% y/y in FY27, driven by continued Cloud momentum and healthy Data Center retention.
As a reminder, our announcement of Data Center’s end-of-life in March 2029 (DC EOL) pulled customer purchases and caused us to recognize a higher proportion of Data Center contract value as upfront term-license revenue in FY26. The year-over-year comparison will weigh on FY27 Data Center revenue and Total revenue growth, but we expect total revenue growth to re-accelerate in FY28 as we lap these effects.
Subscription ARR offers a supplemental view of our underlying business, as it normalizes variable revenue patterns and the timing effects of ASC 606.
Cloud revenue
We expect Cloud revenue to grow approximately 26.0% y/y in FY27,
driven by customers broadening their adoption of our System of Work as they add new users and teams to our core offerings, adopt our Collections to deploy Rovo agents across their workflows, and upgrade to higher-value editions to take advantage of the advanced capabilities and innovation of the Atlassian platform.
We continue to expect migrations from Data Center to Cloud to play out over a multi-year period and to contribute mid-to-high single-digits of Cloud revenue growth in FY27.
In terms of seasonality, we expect 1H growth rates to be higher than 2H due to tougher comparisons in 2H, including the impact from the DX acquisition which closed in Q2’26.
Data Center revenue
We expect Data Center revenue to decline approximately (16.0%) y/y in FY27,
driven by lapping the revenue recognition impacts from the DC EOL announcement, customer purchasing activity that was pulled into FY26 from FY27, continued migrations to Cloud, and lower seat expansion as Data Center customers plan their Cloud migration. These will be partially offset by pricing and Data Center renewals, including customers adopting hybrid deployment strategies, allowing them to migrate over time.
Marketplace and other revenue
We expect Marketplace and other revenue to grow approximately 15.0% y/y,
driven by sales of third-party marketplace apps for our Cloud and Data Center offerings. As a reminder, we currently have a lower Marketplace take rate on the sale of third-party Cloud apps relative to Data Center apps as we incentivize further development on our Forge platform
Gross margin
We expect GAAP gross margin of 84.5% and non-GAAP gross margin of 86.5%,
driven by growing usage of Rovo and hosting costs as more of our business shifts to Cloud. We continue to optimize our cloud infrastructure through our platform investments, and manage AI workloads through our AI Gateway, which matches each AI task to the best-fit model for quality and cost.
Operating margin
We expect GAAP operating margin of 5.0% and non-GAAP operating margin of 25.5%.
FY26 non-GAAP operating margin benefited by approximately four percentage points from the timing of Data Center revenue recognition following the DC EOL announcement. For FY27, we’ve shifted compensation toward cash and away from equity as part of our work to reduce stock-based compensation as a percentage of revenue, which is expected to lower non-GAAP operating margin by approximately three percentage points. Adjusting for these effects, we anticipate an increase in non-GAAP operating margin in FY27 as compared to FY26. We remain focused on GAAP profitability and margin expansion over time while investing judiciously in AI and enterprise sales.
Share count
We expect our net diluted share count to remain relatively flat in FY27 versus FY26. We expect to generate healthy free cash flow, allowing us to opportunistically offset dilution and return capital to stockholders.

In projecting this long-term non-GAAP tax rate, we utilized a three-year financial projection that excludes the direct and indirect income tax effects of the other non-GAAP adjustments reflected above. Additionally, we considered our current operating structure and other factors such as our existing tax positions in various jurisdictions and key legislation in major jurisdictions where we operate. For fiscal years 2027 and 2026, we determined the projected non-GAAP tax rate to be 24%. This fixed long-term projected non-GAAP tax rate eliminates the effects of non-recurring and period specific items which can vary in size and frequency. Examples of the non-recurring and period specific items include but are not limited to changes in the valuation allowance related to deferred tax assets, effects resulting from acquisitions, and unusual or infrequently occurring items. We will periodically re-evaluate this long-term rate, as necessary, for significant events. The rate could be subject to change for a variety of reasons, for example, significant changes in the geographic earnings mix or fundamental tax law changes in major jurisdictions where we operate.
