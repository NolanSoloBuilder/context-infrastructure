CoreWeave Says It Can Hold 2027 Capex Flat at $37B-$39B

AI Business Brief / By Gennaro Cuofano / November 11, 2026

CoreWeave says its 2027 capital spending could stay in line with this year’s $37 billion to $39 billion if it builds only for contracts already signed, according to the transcript of its 10 November earnings call. Chief financial officer Nitin Agrawal said: “Should we choose to focus only on our committed backlog, we would expect CapEx for 2027 to remain in line with our 2026 CapEx investment while revenue and EBITDA grow significantly”.

He added that “more than $9 billion of the required infrastructure is already on our balance sheet, sitting in construction in progress, awaiting placement into service”. The $9.3 billion of construction in progress he reported is about a quarter (24%) of the $38 billion midpoint of the 2026 guide, by our arithmetic.

An evergreen Business Engineer explainer on capital spending and depreciation. It teaches the general idea only and does not discuss CoreWeave.

The key insight: CoreWeave said on its 10 November call that 2027 capital spending would stay in line with 2026’s $37 billion to $39 billion if it builds only for committed backlog. It reported $9.3 billion of construction in progress awaiting service, about a quarter (24%) of the $38 billion midpoint by our arithmetic, plus nearly $5 billion of undrawn DDTL loans.

Update (11 Nov): Bank of America calls the funding “manageable”. BofA analyst Tal Liani kept a Buy rating and a $140 price objective after the results, Benzinga reported. With the stock up 20.9% to $58.92 in early trading Wednesday, the target is 2.4 times that price, about 138% upside. “CoreWeave’s funding needs are manageable, in our view, supported by a diversified set of financing options,” Liani wrote, per Benzinga. The bank projects negative free cash flow of $23 billion this year and $27.3 billion in 2027, and notes third-quarter capex of $14.5 billion was about $2 billion above its estimate. CoreWeave does not expect to tap the unsecured debt market through 2027, funding extra capex with customer prepayments, vendor financing and contract-backed delayed-draw term loans, the report says.

Agrawal framed the flat year as one option. He said: “Looking at 2027 specifically, we retain flexibility in how quickly we execute our growth plan”, and “This means we have levers to control our CapEx”.

He said the company “will deploy capital only where we have robust demand, access to financing on attractive terms, and expected returns that meet our thresholds”. He said holding back would not affect its ability “to meaningfully exceed our ARR target for 2027”.

What Is Already on the Balance Sheet

For 2026 the company raised the low end of its capital-spending guide to $37 billion from $35 billion, its third-quarter release says. Agrawal said third-quarter CapEx was $14.5 billion and that construction in progress fell to $9.3 billion from $11.9 billion “as we deployed significant infrastructure against power that became available late in Q2”.

On funding, he said: “we already have nearly $5 billion available to draw under already raised DDTLs”. At the 2026 midpoint pace of about $0.73 billion a week, that covers about seven weeks of spending, by our arithmetic.

Where the Rest of the Money Comes From

Agrawal pointed to “the approximately $9 billion of prepayments and deposits we expect to collect in the next three months from signed customer contracts”, growth in EBITDA and new asset-backed facilities. He said: “we expect to raise less than $5 billion in incremental parent-level capital through 2027”.

The undrawn loans, the prepayments and the parent-level capital add up to about $19 billion, roughly half of a $38 billion year, by our arithmetic. Intrator said in his remarks that EBITDA less interest, which excludes prepayments, is expected “to more than double in 2027”.

Update (11 Nov, 13:10 UTC): CoreWeave is selling more than GPUs. It announced the general availability of CoreWeave Database, a managed PostgreSQL service that runs on CPU nodes inside customers’ CoreWeave Kubernetes Service clusters, next to their training and inference workloads. It includes high availability with automatic failover, continuous backups with point-in-time recovery, encryption at rest and in transit, and pgvector for vector search, and it is built on the open-source CloudNativePG operator. The pitch is to remove the “cross-cloud hop”: CoreWeave says teams until now ran PostgreSQL themselves or kept it in another cloud. Advaita Bio is the one named customer. The release gives no pricing, so the revenue this adds cannot be sized yet. What it signals is a move to keep more of each AI customer’s stack, and its spending, on the platform that the capex above pays for.

The Structural Read

As we read it, the call turns 2027 spending into a dial rather than a fixed number: the backlog sets a floor near this year’s level, and anything above it depends on demand, financing terms and returns. Which setting CoreWeave picks is not established.

“As market conditions evolve, we can calibrate our future capital commitments accordingly”

Three Implications

FOR SPENDING Agrawal said 2027 CapEx would remain in line with 2026’s if CoreWeave focuses only on committed backlog, while revenue and EBITDA grow significantly.

FOR FUNDING He named nearly $5 billion of undrawn DDTLs, approximately $9 billion of prepayments and deposits over three months and less than $5 billion of new parent-level capital through 2027, about $19 billion together by our arithmetic.

FOR THE BALANCE SHEET Construction in progress fell to $9.3 billion from $11.9 billion in the quarter, a 22% drop by our arithmetic.

The Business Engineer Lens

This story maps onto the Business Engineer framework The AI Capex Map and the State of AI Infrastructure.

The framework says: “the capex race is no longer a corporate spending cycle. It is a sovereign-scale financing problem dressed in tech-company clothing.”

As we read it, a flat-capex option tied to signed contracts is how one operator in that race tries to size its financing problem to its backlog. The framework predates the call and does not discuss it.

What Is Not Established

The 2027 guide was given as “in line with our 2026 CapEx investment”, and the flat case applies only if CoreWeave builds for committed backlog. The documents we read do not show which path the company has chosen. The prepayments are a company expectation for the next three months. The shares, runway and totals here are our arithmetic, not company figures.
