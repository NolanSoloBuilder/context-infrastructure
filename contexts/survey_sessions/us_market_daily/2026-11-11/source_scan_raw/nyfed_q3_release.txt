Household Debt Increased Moderately; Delinquency Transition Rates Remain Flat
November 10, 2026
NEW YORK—The Federal Reserve Bank of New York’s Center for Microeconomic Data today issued its
Quarterly Report  on Household Debt and Credit
. The report shows  total household debt expanded by $334 billion, a 1.8% increase, in Q3 2026, to  $19.1 trillion. The report is based on data from the New York  Fed’s nationally representative
Consumer Credit Panel
. It includes a one-page summary of key takeaways and their supporting  data points.
The New York Fed also issued an accompanying
Liberty Street Economics
blog post examining differences in the rise  in credit card delinquencies by neighborhood income and the role of changing  lending standards over the last several years.
“Household  debt levels rose moderately, led by increases in mortgage and home equity line of credit originations” said Donghoon Lee, Economic Research Advisor at the New  York Fed. “Although delinquency transition rates for housing debts have crept  up, the overall credit quality of these loans remains strong.”
Mortgage  balances rose by $257 billion in the third quarter and totaled $13.4 trillion at the end of September. Home  equity lines of credit (HELOC) balances rose by $23 billion totaling $481  billion, the largest increase in HELOC balances observed since 2005. Credit card balances rose by $22 billion and stood at $1.29 trillion, and auto loan  balances increased by $21 billion to $1.73 trillion. Student loan balances edged  up by $5 billion and stood at $1.66 trillion.
The  pace of mortgage originations grew notably with $595 billion newly originated in  Q3 2026. Auto loan originations remained at a high rate; there  were $208 billion in new auto loans appearing on credit reports during  the third quarter. Aggregate limits on credit cards continued to rise,  with a $115 billion uptick in the third quarter. HELOC  limits rose by $31 billion, continuing an expansion in HELOCs that began  in 2022.
Aggregate delinquency rates worsened in Q3 2026, with 5.0%  of outstanding debt in some stage of delinquency, up 0.3 percentage points from  the previous quarter. Transition into early delinquency continued to tick up  for auto loans and mortgages but improved slightly for credit cards. Transition  rates into serious delinquency slightly improved for credit cards and student  loans, while those for auto loans and HELOCs remained flat. For mortgages, the  transition rate into serious delinquency worsened but remains low by historical  standards.

About the Report
The Federal Reserve Bank of New York’s
Household Debt and  Credit Report
provides unique data and insight into the credit conditions  and activity of U.S. consumers. Based on data from the New York Fed’s
Consumer Credit  Panel
, a nationally representative sample drawn from anonymized Equifax  credit data, the report provides a quarterly snapshot of household trends in  borrowing and indebtedness, including data about mortgages, student loans,  credit cards, auto loans, and delinquencies. The report aims to help community  groups, small businesses, state and local governments, and the public to better  understand, monitor, and respond to trends in borrowing and indebtedness at the  household level. Sections of the report are presented as interactive graphs on  the New York Fed’s
Household Debt and Credit Report webpage
and the  full report is available for download.
